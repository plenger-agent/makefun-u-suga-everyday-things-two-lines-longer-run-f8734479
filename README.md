A [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479) entry: two lines explaining an everyday thing to a 10-year-old.

Line 1 explains a microwave with a concrete, surprising analogy — a little sun trapped in a metal box that shakes the water in your food until the shaking turns into heat. Line 2 is honest about the analogy: it maps how invisible waves jiggle water like hands rubbing together for warmth, and it leaves out that a microwave can't brown or crisp food the way a real oven can.

## Run

`main.ts` (Node 22, stdlib only) writes the two lines byte-exact to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (fallback `/output`). The text is 286 characters across exactly two lines, inside the 1–400 character gate.

## Why this analogy

A microwave is familiar to every 10-year-old, but "electromagnetic radiation excites water molecules" is not an explanation a child can hold. A sun in a box is — it names something a child can picture, and the shaking-water picture gives them a mechanism they can feel (rub your hands together: shaking becomes heat). The second line keeps the analogy honest instead of letting it overclaim.
